# Border Empires — Game Mechanics Reference

Status: canonical reference

Canonical "how the game actually works" reference for agents working on AI, gameplay, balance, or anything that needs grounded knowledge of the rules. Surveyed 2026-05-14 against the rewrite stack (`apps/simulation`, `apps/realtime-gateway`, `packages/shared`, `packages/game-domain`, `packages/sim-protocol`, `packages/client-protocol`, `packages/client`). The legacy `packages/server` stack was removed in commit `ec4614d` (PR #264); only the rewrite is authoritative.

For the design-level view (loops, pacing, sources/sinks, retention), see `docs/core-loop.md`.

## 1. Map and spatial structure

- **Coordinate system**: square grid, integer `(x, y)`. World wraps on both axes at `WORLD_WIDTH` × `WORLD_HEIGHT`. `packages/shared/src/exposure/exposure.ts:5-10`
- **Neighbors**: 4 cardinal directions (N, E, S, W) only. No diagonals for gameplay. `packages/shared/src/exposure/exposure.ts:5-10, 65-66`
- **No chunk/region grid exists.** The world operates per-tile. Tile metadata can carry cluster tags (`FERTILE_PLAINS`, `TITANIUM_HILLS`) but those are not aggregation structures. `packages/shared/src/types.ts:7, 439-444`
- **Terrain types**: `LAND` (claimable, passable), `SEA` / `COASTAL_SEA` (barrier, not claimable; combat blocked except via dock links/aether bridges), `MOUNTAIN` (barrier, mutable via aether abilities). Only `LAND` is claimable. `packages/shared/src/types.ts:1, 15, 200`
- **Fog of war**: per-player visibility. Tiles carry an optional `fogged` flag. Every tile in a player's current authoritative reach border contributes fixed radius-one vision, so all reachable ground remains visible even without frontier tiles and the reach edge reveals one extra tile outward; normal frontier, settled-land, allied, town, structure, dock, and temporary sources still stack. Observatory (Aether Tower) structures extend vision radius and shield their OWNER's tiles (never unowned or third-party land) within `OBSERVATORY_PROTECTION_RADIUS` from hostile tile-targeted Aether abilities -- Aether Purge, Aether EMP, Create/Remove Mountain and Aether Bridge landings -- while active, with a shot left (see Aether Tower shots) and not dormant. Enforced in the simulation against full world state (`isTileShieldedByEnemyObservatory`; the shielding rule itself is `apps/simulation/src/aether-shield/aether-shield.ts`), so a tower the caster can't see still blocks; Siphon uses the same rule. Each online player is pushed `AETHER_SHIELDS` (on change, and on connect; the gateway replays the latest to extra tabs) listing the other players' shielding towers whose land they can see, which the client's protection-field tint and target greying read (`aether-shield-broadcast.ts`). Fogged tiles keep the state they had when last witnessed (the FOG delta freezes them), and both client renderers draw their remembered buildings -- towns, structures, forts, siege camps, Relay Beacons, Aether Towers, AFCs with their docked modules, docks, watchtowers, waystations -- as last seen but standing still: no animation, no build in progress, dimmed lights, under the fog (`client-map-3d/client-map-3d-remembered-buildings.ts`, `client-remembered-structures-2d/`). `apps/simulation/src/reach-border-vision.ts`, `packages/shared/src/types.ts:203`, `packages/game-domain/src/server-game-constants/server-game-constants.ts:49-51`
- **Line of sight**: vision footprints are filtered per ray from the source tile. A mountain strictly between source and target blocks everything behind it (the mountain itself stays visible); forest lets a ray continue only `FOREST_VISION_RANGE` (1) tile past the first forest tile it crosses, the source's own tile excepted, and a tile whose live terrain is SEA/COASTAL_SEA never counts as forest (season worldgen carves sea channels through the static forest map); hills add `HILLS_VISION_BONUS`. Relay Beacon (and Siege Battery outpost) vision rings ignore forest -- a beacon sees over the canopy -- but are still blocked by mountains. Territory, town, Aether Tower and other sources keep forest occlusion. `apps/simulation/src/vision-line-of-sight.ts`, `apps/simulation/src/vision-footprint-table.ts`, `apps/simulation/src/runtime-outpost-vision.ts`
- **Aether Tower shots**: every ability cast from an Aether Tower (or a Watchtower Engine / Concordance Resonator acting as one) -- Reveal Empire Stats, Survey Sweep, Aether Purge, Aether EMP, Aether Wall, Aether Bridge, Siphon, Create/Remove Mountain, Retort Transmutation and Counterphase Emitter's suppression -- spends one of that tower's shots instead of starting a per-ability cooldown. Each tower holds 3 shots (`AETHER_TOWER_BASE_SHOTS`), and each shot comes back 24h after it was fired, so a tower fires at most 3 times in any rolling 24h. `observatoryShotsMult` multiplies the count: AFC Resonance Governor (`afc-oracle-2`) and legacy Hidden Hand ×2 (6 shots), legacy Oracle State ×1.5. Capacity rounds to whole shots (Oracle State alone 5, with Hidden Hand 9). The tower stores only its fire times still inside the window (`observatory.shotsUsedAt`, at most 12) and remaining shots are derived on read with the owner's current capacity, so nothing ticks however many towers exist and gaining or losing a shots effect, or a capture, applies at once. A tower shields while it has a shot left (`aetherTowerHasShot`; a tower with no shot history yet falls back to its old `cooldownUntil`). `observatory.cooldownUntil` is still written on each shot as the earliest time an empty tower fires again (absent while it has a shot), a hint for readers that can't know the owner's capacity: the AI planner, other players' clients and the 3D tower badge. Casting picks the in-range tower with most shots left, then the closest, so a nearer tower doesn't spend its last shot (and its shield) while another has spare; Reveal Empire Stats picks the fullest tower. Siphon spends its shot when it starts; ending it costs nothing more. A captured tower keeps its spent shots. `packages/shared/src/aether-tower-shots/aether-tower-shots.ts`, `apps/simulation/src/observatory-shots/`
- **Siphon (Observatory siphon mode)**: casting Siphon (tech `logistics`) on an enemy town/resource tile locks the caster's nearest ready Observatory into *siphon mode* (`observatory.siphon`) and stamps every enemy town/resource tile in the 3x3 with a `sabotage` that points back at that tower (`observatoryTileKey`). While it lasts: drained towns produce nothing, and each drained RESOURCE tile's slots count toward the caster's slot supply instead of the owner's (same resource, same count incl. the owner's boosts), so the owner's newest structures may go dormant and the caster's may wake up. There is no timer. It ends when the caster sends `CANCEL_SIPHON` from the tower, one of the victim's own Observatories is shielding a drained tile again (active, with a shot left, not dormant -- checked on the tower's tile write and by a periodic sweep, since a shot coming back or power returning writes no tile), the caster's tower stops being an active tower they own (destroyed, captured, removed, switched off), or a drained tile changes owner. Tiles their owner's Observatory is shielding right now can't be siphoned, but land whose tower is out of shots or dormant can -- the siphon then ends as soon as that tower is back online. Draining the victim's CRYSTAL can keep their tower dormant; they then have to retake the tile, find CRYSTAL elsewhere, or take out the caster's tower. A tower in siphon mode can't cast other abilities; starting the siphon spent one of its shots, and ending it costs nothing more. `PURGE_SIPHON` stays rejected — the victim's counter is an Observatory. `apps/simulation/src/runtime-siphon-command-handlers.ts`, `apps/simulation/src/siphon-mode/`, `packages/shared/src/siphon-mode/siphon-mode.ts`
  Frontier resource Siphon transfers only working extractor supply, preserving the source Manifest requirement. Undeveloped sites supply nothing. Changes to stolen supply settle and refresh dependent resource-trade agreements; cancelling the drain restores the original owner’s slot.
- **Docks**: Maritime Supremacy counts settled dock tiles. Docks are also used for cross-island movement and linked-dock vision. Dock-to-dock attacks display amphibious troop carriers on both map renderers: a sea approach, opening bow ramp, infantry disembarking into combat, and a beached carrier through the end of the battle. Undefended frontier dock landings unload and hold without spawning defenders or gunfire. This is cosmetic and does not change combat costs, timing or outcomes; overland attacks keep their ordinary approach. Shared landing math and both carrier renderers live in `packages/client/src/client-dock-landing-ground/`.
  - **Beach landings**: a dock crossing (ATTACK or EXPAND) from a dock you own may target the dock it links to *or* any coastal LAND tile within `DOCK_LANDING_RADIUS` (1, `packages/shared/src/dock-landing/dock-landing.ts`) of that linked dock -- its *landing ground*. Coastal means water on at least one cardinal side (worldgen's COASTAL_SAND test); inland land beside the dock is not landing ground. Crossings skip the reach gate and Aether walls as before, and a failed assault still forfeits the origin dock (`runtime-lock-resolution-lost-origin.ts`) -- a Palisade on the dock is the counter. Rule: `packages/shared/src/dock-landing/dock-landing.ts`; the simulation builds a static index once per world (`RuntimeState.dockLanding`), so finding the launching dock for a target is a lookup, not a territory scan (`apps/simulation/src/runtime/runtime-crossing.ts`).
  - **Sea-lane supply**: a landing tile and the dock(s) that can land on it are supply neighbours for encirclement, so a beachhead stays supplied while you hold a dock it was launched from (or another dock linked to the same harbour). When a dock changes hands, its former owner's frontier on the far landing ground is re-checked from the landing side and released at once if it no longer reaches supply -- independent of the empire-wide BFS cap (`releaseStrandedSeaLaneLandings`, `apps/simulation/src/runtime-encirclement-application.ts`; counters `sim_stranded_frontier_sea_lane_checks_total` / `_released_total`).
  - **Client** (both renderers): always on, the viewer's coast (owned or in reach) is tinted warning orange wherever a dock held by a hostile player (not an ally or live truce partner; barbarians count) can land on it -- a neutral far dock is no threat until taken (`client-dock-landing-ground-threat.ts`). Selecting a dock tints the landing ground at both ends of its sea lanes in sea-foam (selecting a beach shows its harbour's ground). Each landing tile gets an edge, a glow band on the water off its sea side(s) and a chevron pointing ashore (animated in true-3D, `client-map-3d-dock-landing-overlay/dock-landing-marker-layer.ts`; static in 2D), and the tile panel names the docks whose crossings can land on a tile (`packages/client/src/client-dock-landing-ground/`, `client-map-3d-dock-landing-overlay/`). The harbour itself shows its landing ground at all times (both renderers): the dock tile carries the quay and every LAND beach of its landing ground carries a landing slipway facing the water, shrunk to the waterline beside another structure or resource and left out on a town, natural wonder or AFC tile. Purely visual -- placement `packages/client/src/client-harbour-slipways/`, models `client-map-3d-harbour/` + `client-map-3d-dock-overlay.ts`, 2D glyph `client-harbour-slipways-2d.ts`. Origin selection, the muster attack gate and the waypoint planner all accept landing ground. The AI's direct frontier planner still targets linked docks only; AI muster flags use beach landings through the shared muster pathing. Barbarians holding a dock land on beaches too: the barbarian planner turns on `dockBeachLandings` in the shared frontier planner (`apps/simulation/src/ai/frontier-scoring.ts` `dockCrossingTargetKeysForOrigin`), reading terrain from its own tile view.
- **Map names (player names and island names)**. *Player names*: zoomed far out (`state.zoom` at or below 14, crossfading to island names by 18), each player's name is written across each connected territory made up of their garrisoned and frontier land, provided it contains a fully owned 3x3 block. The text follows the "core" (tiles whose 8 neighbours are all held by the same owner) along its long axis and bends with it. Letters spread out and grow as the core lengthens and thickens. This is client-only, computed from known tiles: `packages/client/src/client-map-name-labels/client-territory-name-layout.ts`. *Island names*: an island is an 8-connected run of non-sea tiles (LAND or MOUNTAIN, wrapping at world edges). Every island with at least `ISLAND_NAME_MIN_LAND_TILES` (9) LAND tiles has a seeded default name from the town name cultures (`generatedIslandName`). A player who garrisons every LAND tile of an island can rename it with `NAME_ISLAND`. Players see islands as "landmasses": the client asks once, with a "name this landmass" dialog, the first time it sees the player holding a whole landmass (also on the next login if it happened while away), remembered per world seed + landmass in the account's server-saved hint state (`client-landmass-name-prompt.ts`). Names are 3-28 characters and checked against the shared place-name blocklist. A rival's earlier name on that island is replaced. The chosen name is *held* at 100%, *contested* while the namer holds at least 60% (`ISLAND_NAME_CONTESTED_MIN_SHARE`), and *fading* below that. A fading name reverts to the default `ISLAND_NAME_FADE_MS` (24h) after it began fading, unless the namer climbs back to 60% first. Claims persist on the namer's player record (`islandNames`). The sim pushes the `ISLAND_NAMES` label list (centre, long-axis angle, span, thickness, name, status) on connect and whenever what that viewer may see changes (checked every ~2s). Fog: a chosen name, its namer and its held / contested / fading state go only to the namer and players holding any tile on that island; everyone else gets the generated name (`islandLabelsForViewer`). The gateway replays the latest list to newly opened tabs. Island labels show only once their centre tile is explored. Names are dark cartographer's ink and always fit inside the tiles they belong on, never overflowing them: a player's name stays on their own explored territory, an island's name on the island's explored garrisons (the namer's own for a chosen name, anyone's for a generated one, placed along the core of that garrisoned area), one tile in from unexplored ground; letter height is capped by the distance to the nearest outside tile, and a name that can't fit at a readable size is not drawn (`client-name-path-fit.ts`, `client-region-name-layout.ts`, `client-map-name-placements.ts`). In true-3D they are drawn into a world-aligned canvas laid on a mesh that follows the rendered ground (`RENDER_ORDER.groundNames`, depth-tested, so structures and units stand in front, and fog and storm clouds cover them): `packages/client/src/client-map-3d-name-decal/`. The 2D map draws the same labels in the runtime loop's overlay pass: `packages/shared/src/island-names/island-names.ts`, `apps/simulation/src/island-names/`, `packages/client/src/client-map-name-labels/`.
- **Frontier supply (encirclement) and out-of-reach frontier**. *Encirclement*: a FRONTIER tile must reach one of its owner's SETTLED tiles, or one of its owner's FRONTIER dock tiles, through the owner's own frontier (8-neighbour, plus active Aether bridge links and dock sea lanes -- see Beach landings above). A cut-off tile decays instantly (no timer). It is re-checked when a nearby tile changes owner (combat, abandon, Aether Lance, sky dock bombardment, Create Mountain), when an EXPAND/ATTACK is launched from it (a cut-off origin decays and the action fails with `ORIGIN_CUT_OFF`), and when any player's camera chunk covers it (gateway forwards `SUBSCRIBE_CHUNKS` once per chunk per login as the non-durable `CHECK_STRANDED_REGION`; barbarian tiles are skipped). *Out-of-reach frontier*: FRONTIER outside its owner's reach does **not** decay (the old `OUT_OF_REACH` timer was removed; the sim strips any persisted one at boot). It can't be settled (SETTLE stays reach-gated, except towns/docks/captured buildings), a captured town/dock beyond reach auto-settles at capture if affordable, and while supply-connected it remains a valid origin -- so its only weakness is encirclement: cut the chain anywhere and everything past the cut is lost at once. The client flags only the fragile part of that chain (both renderers): beyond-reach land that hangs on a single FRONTIER tile (a cut vertex of the supply graph, nearest supply when cuts nest) gets a small ring on that tile (slowly brightening and fading at the pulses' pace) and a pale supply line from the nearest supply tile (settled land or a frontier dock), through the cut, out to the land's tips along the fewest-steps supply path, with faint pulses flowing outward (no arrowhead). A beach landing hangs on its home dock across the sea: that dock gets the ring even when settled, and the line follows the dock's sea route. Land with more than one way back is not drawn; capturing a tile can make a loop's other arm fragile, and the marks update to match. Client-only, mirrors the sim's supply rule: `packages/client/src/client-frontier-supply/`, `packages/client/src/client-map-3d-frontier-supply-overlay.ts`. `apps/simulation/src/encirclement/encirclement.ts`, `apps/simulation/src/stranded-frontier/stranded-frontier-cleanup.ts`, `apps/simulation/src/runtime-reach-update/runtime-reach-out-of-reach.ts`, `apps/simulation/src/runtime-out-of-reach-auto-settle/runtime-out-of-reach-auto-settle.ts`, `apps/realtime-gateway/src/ephemeral-sim-commands/ephemeral-sim-commands.ts`
- **Reach anchors**: only SETTLED towns and AFCs (`TOWN_REACH_RADIUS`, 3), active Relay Beacons (`OUTPOST_REACH_RADIUS`, 5) and owned docks (`DOCK_REACH_RADIUS`, 1) project reach; barbarians project none. Siege Batteries, Siege Towers and Dread Towers are combat structures and hold **no** reach, so a battery left standing after a front's beacons fall does not keep the rival's ground under its owner's border. Existing border slots that only a siege battery held are dropped on the next restart by the boot reconcile (`runtime-reach-border-restore.ts`). `apps/simulation/src/runtime/runtime-reach-anchors.ts`, client preview mirror `packages/client/src/client-reach-overlay/client-reach-overlay-anchors.ts`
- **Reach border persists across restarts**: the reach border is history-dependent -- where two players' anchors cover the same tile, whoever claimed it first keeps it while one of their live anchors still covers it -- so it is saved in every snapshot (`reachBorder`, delta-encoded per owner, `apps/simulation/src/reach-border-snapshot/reach-border-snapshot.ts`) and every anchor change persists its exact diff as a sim-internal `REACH_BORDER_CHANGED` event, which startup recovery replays on top of the last snapshot (never sent to the gateway, never replay-cached, neither live nor when command history is hydrated after a restart). At boot `restoreReachBorder` (`apps/simulation/src/runtime-reach-update/runtime-reach-border-restore.ts`) drops entries whose owner no longer covers the tile with a live anchor (including unpersisted Aether bridge reach), then replays live anchors earliest-activated first, which only fills unclaimed slots and runs the rival-SETTLED contest. A snapshot written before persistence falls back to: every owned tile its owner's own anchors cover is that owner's reach. A diff needs a known base, so a boot with no saved border (first deploy, bootstrap state) and a season rollover each force a checkpoint of the rebuilt border straight away (`checkpointSoon`, which logs a skip or failure). Boot logs `[reachBorderRestore]` with `source`, `restored`, `dropped`, `borderSize`. The runtime mutates its one live border in place (`grantAnchorToBorderInPlace` / `reassessBorderOnAnchorDeactivationInPlace`) rather than cloning it per anchor change.

## 2. Players and factions

- **Towns are exclusively world-generated — zero are player-founded.** World gen seeds `max(70, 180 * worldScale)` unowned/neutral towns across the map at season start (`packages/game-domain/src/server-worldgen-towns.ts:50`), each already at some `populationTier` with a `MARKET`/`FARMING` type. A player only ever *acquires* one of these pre-existing town tiles (SETTLE if it's still neutral, ATTACK if another player holds it); there is no command that creates a town where none existed. The one other town record a player ends up with is their single free starting SETTLEMENT-tier tile, and that's fabricated by the *system's* spawn/respawn assignment (`apps/simulation/src/runtime-respawn-helpers.ts:168`), not by any player action. See §4's SETTLE note for why SETTLE itself can't create a town from bare land.
- **Season-start placement uses the fair-spawn roster**: worldgen keeps the roster its acceptance check computed (`computeFairSpawnSitesForWorldgen`, 50 sites, starter economy required) and every season-start empire claims from it: the AI empires, plus any human slots a caller seeds (production seeds none). Each claim takes the open site farthest from all starts placed so far, where open means unowned and at least 10 tiles from another start. An AI falls back to the seeded 50/35-tile search only once no roster site is open; a human slot with no open site fails worldgen (`apps/simulation/src/season-seed-world-player-spawn.ts`). Live joins and respawns go through `chooseStarterSpawn` (`apps/simulation/src/runtime-respawn-helpers.ts`), which recomputes a roster of the same kind of sites from live tiles, so a site an AI took at season start, and land near it, is no longer offered.
- **AFC landing site**: a fresh spawn/respawn (and the pre-AFC migration grant) lands its Automated Fabrication Complex only on a tile with no water (SEA/COASTAL_SEA) on any of its 8 neighbours, falling back to any tile only if none qualifies (`hasWaterNeighbor` in `packages/game-domain/src/server-worldgen-fair-spawn-sites.ts`). Unlike the water rule, there is no fallback for obstructions: the AFC tile and all 8 neighbours must be free of towns, docks, resources, buildings, and other landmark footprints (`isAfcSiteClear`, same file), so players settle those themselves. On landing, mountains in the 3x3 footprint are flattened to LAND and forest is cleared from all 9 tiles (`apps/simulation/src/afc-landing-footprint/afc-landing-footprint.ts`). Forest is likewise cleared on every town and dock tile (`clearForestOnTownOrDockTile`). The forest clearing is not persisted: both simulation and client re-derive it from `tile.afc` / `tile.town` / `tile.dockId` (`packages/shared/src/forest-terrain/forest-clearing.ts`). Visually, while the viewer's own join drop is still hiding the AFC, both renderers keep drawing the original footprint mountains and forest, so they vanish at touchdown (`packages/client/src/client-afc-join-drop/client-afc-join-drop-state.ts`); other players' AFC footprints change immediately.
- **Landmark building footprints**: no new buildings, including forts, can go on an AFC or relic center or any of its eight neighbors. AFC modules still dock normally. New AFCs need a clear footprint and cannot overlap another AFC or relic site. Existing buildings are not removed.
- **AFC loss and recovery**: losing the last AFC suspends new construction and removes AFC Coin and manpower contributions. Humans and AI must issue `BUILD_AFC` on empty owned LAND (FRONTIER allowed outside capture holds) with a clear 3×3 footprint, separated from other AFC footprints. Replacement price is **20 + 10 × max(0, global AFC losses − 1) Coin**; the first global loss quotes 20. Capture or destruction of any AFC increments the campaign count once, including recaptures. One paid delivery per player takes **60 seconds**, with its price locked at ordering. Loss/obstruction of the site or securing another AFC cancels and fully refunds the order; retrying starts a fresh countdown. Orders and the global count survive restart. The recovery popup offers a landing site, continued fighting or **Withdraw and redeploy**, which forfeits territory, treasury and fielded manpower for an opening landing while retaining House knowledge. Fresh joins and full-elimination recovery retain their existing opening landing. Module slots and capture plunder: `docs/manifest-full-plan.md` §4. Implementation: `apps/simulation/src/afc-owned-site/`.
- **Tech-locked resource builds stay visible**: on a resource tile, the same-tile builds (Hydrogarden, Umbrite Rig, Mine) are shown greyed out instead of hidden when the missing tech is the only thing blocking them: an unresearched tech is named with a "View Manifest" link that opens its detail card, and a researched AFC module not installed on an active AFC says to install it. Any other blocker (a structure on the tile, no free slot) keeps the row hidden, as do all other tech-locked builds. An unresearched Umbrite Rig never appears this way, because Umbrite Rigging Module is also what reveals Umbrite on the map. `packages/client/src/client-tile-action-support/client-tech-locked-tile-action.ts`
- **AFC Modules tab**: a top-down diagram shows all eight sockets, clockwise from east, in the same order as the 3D attachments. House copies are movable; captured copies stay with their AFC. Incoming deliveries reserve space (their final socket is assigned when they land). On an active AFC you own, an empty socket opens call-down controls for unlocked module types missing here, including your movable copies installed on another AFC. Calling down moves that copy immediately and takes one minute to arrive; it does not create a duplicate.
- **AFC Upgrades**: campaign domain choices have their own global management panel, linked from owned AFC overviews. Phones expand details within the comparison list; desktop shows details beside the cards. A ready count stays in navigation, and clickable ready badges appear above visible active owned AFCs in both renderers after landing. Readiness uses current resources and prerequisites. Existing costs and rewards remain in force; free Manifest-tier choices and the thirty new rewards are still the [specialization proposal](afc-specializations-plan.md).
- **AFC landing clears barbarians**: right after the footprint is prepared (and before the AFC tile is written), every barbarian-owned tile inside the new AFC's land-gated reach disk (`TOWN_REACH_RADIUS`, 3) is released to neutral, using the same release shape as a barbarian walk, so the AFC's reach grant then auto-claims it FRONTIER like any neutral ground. Tiles with a combat lock or pending settlement are skipped. Applies to the fresh-spawn and both respawn paths, not the pre-AFC migration grant. Logged as `afc_landing_barbarians_cleared` (`apps/simulation/src/afc-landing-footprint/afc-landing-barbarian-clear.ts`). Placement itself: barbarian tiles are `SETTLED`, so they already count toward `minSpawnDistance` (50, 20, 10 tiles on the successive passes); only when every distance pass fails does the first 0-distance fallback pass additionally prefer a site with no barbarian within `BARBARIAN_SPAWN_AVOID_RADIUS` (5). The unflagged 0-distance pass and the last-resort loop accept any site, so a spawn is never refused over barbarians (`apps/simulation/src/spawn-placement/barbarian-proximity.ts`). The release drops any `economicStructure` on the cleared tile (a capture re-stamps its owner to `barbarian-1`).
- **AFC landing keeps clear of rival reach**: every spawn/respawn search pass except the very last also requires that no other player's reach border sits within `SPAWN_RIVAL_REACH_CLEARANCE` (the AFC's `TOWN_REACH_RADIUS` disk plus one ring, 4) of the site; rally spawns included (`avoidRivalReach` in `apps/simulation/src/spawn-placement/spawn-placement.ts`). The ordinary 50/20/10-tile passes already imply this, so it only bites on the crowded-map 0-distance fallbacks and on rally spawns, which land 3 tiles from the inviter. Rival reach outranks barbarians: the landing wipes barbarians, but nothing frees a rival's reach. When only the last pass can place a spawn, the simulation logs `spawn_map_nearly_full`, the signal to size the next season's world up. Two guarantees cover that last-resort case: a landed AFC (its `afc.landedAt` is set by the spawn/respawn paths and stripped on capture) always gets its own 3x3 footprint as reach, over any rival's live defense (`AFC_LANDING_GUARANTEED_REACH_RADIUS`, `ReachAnchor.guaranteedRadius` in `packages/shared/src/reach/reach.ts`; position-based, so the boot reseed reproduces it), and every owned AFC sees `AFC_VISION_RADIUS` (4) around itself regardless of owned land (`apps/simulation/src/runtime-town-vision/runtime-town-vision.ts`).
- **Player count**: no hard limit. AI players are flagged `isAi: true` in the player definition. Prod first season is seeded island-heavy with 5 AI players. `packages/shared/src/types.ts:376-413`
- **No civ/faction asymmetry**. Every player shares the same stat-mod fields (`attack`, `defense`, `income`, `vision`), the same tech tree, and the same ability catalog. Differentiation is per-player via tech progress and strategic-resource control, not faction baselines. `packages/shared/src/types.ts:387-388`
- **Barbarians (rewrite model, post-`f5ba210` / PR #256)**: not dynamic agents. Implemented as **tiles owned by player `"barbarian-1"`**, with 80 FRONTIER tiles seeded at world gen far from player spawns (`apps/simulation/src/season-seed-world.ts`, `seed-state.ts:183`). Behavior:
  - **Player-facing name: "Planetary Defense"** (`PLANETARY_DEFENSE_DISPLAY_NAME` in `packages/shared/src/player-display-name.ts`) — the remnant of the planet's own defense force after it was prepared for the planetary games. Internal ids/code stay "barbarian". The client shows dark-grey-armored soldiers patrolling each of its tiles (`client-map-3d-planetary-defense-overlay.ts`, 2D: `client-map-render-planetary-defense-overlay.ts`); in 3D they stand still only with Reduce Motion on or on a device whose smoothed frame time stays above 50ms (~20fps) for 10s (`client-map-3d-unit-motion-gate.ts`), and its side of any battle squad uses the same armor tint.
  - **Wake when seen**: a barb tile may act only while some non-barb player can see it in their fog of war (`computeBarbTilesSeenByAnyPlayer`, `apps/simulation/src/runtime-barb-activation-vision.ts`, which reads the same coverage the client's fog is built from: territory radius, FRONTIER halo, town rings, outposts, observatories, watchtower reveals, allied vision). Unseen barbs cost nothing.
  - **Independent tiles**: each seen barb tile takes its own turn (`apps/simulation/src/ai/system-job-barbarian-planner.ts`). The tile that has waited longest goes first and is analysed on its own, so an ATTACK elsewhere never hides another tile's walk. A tile has at most one action in flight, then rests `BARBARIAN_TILE_REST_MS` (15s) counted from when the action **settles** (combat resolved / claim resolved / rejected), not from when it was issued. A seen barb tile should never stand still for more than about a minute.
  - **Attack budget**: all barbarians together may start at most `BARBARIAN_ATTACKS_PER_MINUTE` (12) attacks per rolling minute (attacks cost ~15ms of sim main thread each, walks ~0.5ms). A tile that wants to attack with the budget spent walks into neutral land instead.
  - **Walk / multiply**: when a barb tile wins an ATTACK/EXPAND (vs a player), per-tile progress accumulates in `SimulationRuntime.barbarianTileProgress`. Progress gain: +2 if the target tile held a resource / town / fort / dock / siege, otherwise +1 (`barbarianProgressGain`, `runtime-barbarian-walk.ts`). At `BARBARIAN_MULTIPLY_THRESHOLD` (5) the source tile stays barb (multiply); below it the source releases to neutral (walk). Progress is dropped when a tile leaves barbarian ownership by any route.
  - **Territory cap**: `MAX_BARBARIAN_TILES` (100). At the cap a win walks instead of multiplying, and the planner alternates between normal actions for seen tiles and releasing a tile nobody can see (`UNCAPTURE_TILE`), so barbarians in view keep moving while the faction shrinks.
  - **Clearing them with a flag**: a human muster flag on ADVANCE clears hostile tiles within **10 steps** of itself, where a step moves through the owner's own land or neutral land (water, mountains and other players' land block the way, so an enemy across a lake or behind a range is out of range even if close in a straight line; a dock or aether-bridge crossing counts as one step, and a dock crossing reaches the linked dock's whole landing ground). Hostile means barbarians and any other player who is not an ally or truced (those are never targets, and are not a road either). Enemies touching its territory are attacked (up to 3 fights at once, launched from owned tiles within 10 steps through its own land); ones that don't are approached by expanding across neutral land toward the nearest (a barbarian wins a tie). Once something hostile has been engaged and nothing is left in range, the flag returns to HOLD and an `AREA_CLEARED` entry lands in the player's event log / Activity Feed. Human flags fire on a 1s ticker (`tickWatchedMusterTiles`); AI flags stay on the 30s territory-automation sweep. **Effort**: Set Advance first asks for an effort level (Normal / Extra / Double, `client-muster-advance-effort/`), sent as `SET_MUSTER.commitManpower` against `MUSTER_ATTACK_COST`; each auto-attack on a SETTLED target commits that ratio (at most Double, `ADVANCE_MAX_EFFORT_MULTIPLIER`) times the target's own floor, capped at the manpower cap but never below the floor (`advanceFlagCommitForTarget`); the flag saves up for the full commitment (reported as insufficient manpower) before firing and raises its own capLevel to hold it (`musterCapLevelToFit`). Held FRONTIER targets use `flagCommitForTarget` and plain FRONTIER ignores effort. The flag's badge shows its capacity, and its commit tab slider runs from Normal to Double. See `apps/simulation/src/runtime-muster-tick/muster-advance-fire.ts` and `muster-advance-clear.ts`.
  - **Hunt Planetary Defense**: a flag order (`SET_MUSTER` ADVANCE with `hunt: true`, stored as `MusterState.hunt`) that is ADVANCE restricted to Planetary Defense: its attack search and its expand-toward search only take barbarian tiles (rivals are neither targets nor approached), and the tile in its `targetX/Y` (the one the player clicked) is attacked first while it is still barbarian. Because every search re-scans, a patrol that walks away is chased, and because the server fires the ATTACK on its next muster tick (about a second; its march time is added to the lock as for any auto-fire), a patrol on the flag's border is target-locked and can't launch its own walk while the company marches (one further off is approached by expanding first, so it can still move). Like any ADVANCE it reports back only once it has engaged something (`clearing`): a hunt that can't reach a patrol waits with "No Planetary Defense within range". Once engaged and with no Planetary Defense left within range -- and no barbarian attack still in flight on the player's own land within `ADVANCE_MAX_RANGE_TILES`, since a patrol mid-capture stands on no tile (`barbarianAttackInRangeResolvesAt`) -- it returns to HOLD with a "Planetary Defense cleared" `AREA_CLEARED` entry. Any later SET_MUSTER without `hunt` ends it; re-ordering a hunting flag keeps its `clearing` state, and a new hunt order skips the cooldown left by the previous order (a hunting flag whose `nextActionAt` no longer matches its cooldown, and isn't in flight, was re-ordered since). Client: flags offer **Hunt Planetary Defense** (HOLD and plain ADVANCE; a hunting flag offers Set Advance instead); a manual attack on a barbarian tile turns the flag the ordinary attack routing picked into a hunt aimed at that tile -- only when that flag is holding or already hunting, isn't funding a manual attack still marching, and reaches the target the way the server hunt does (BFS over its own land, king moves, within `MUSTER_ADVANCE_RANGE_STEPS`, dock crossing onto the target; `client-muster-hunt-reach.ts`) -- otherwise the attack goes the ordinary way -- instead of arming a client-side march (which used to arrive after the patrol had left, failing with "target must be enemy-controlled land"); and the player's own tile being captured by Planetary Defense offers **Hunt Planetary Defense** as its main action, sending the nearest flag that passes the same checks and has at least `MUSTER_ATTACK_COST` staged hunting with the tile as its first target (`client-muster-hunt/`, `muster-advance-fire.ts`).
  - **March length cap**: a MARCH target must be within `MUSTER_MARCH_MAX_DISTANCE_TILES` (30) tiles of its flag (toroidal Chebyshev). The client shows advice instead of sending a longer one ("raise a muster flag closer: troops take much longer to walk across the map than it takes to muster next to the fight"); the server rejects it with `MUSTER_MARCH_TOO_FAR` and the same text as a backstop. Flags set before the cap existed keep their order until they go stale or stop short (see Unexplored march targets). The real route may be longer than the straight line -- a target across a strait is reached through the nearest dock or the player's aether bridge -- so the route search floods up to `MUSTER_MARCH_MAX_ROUTE_STEPS` (60, +2 slack) steps from the target (a dock link or bridge is one step) rather than only the straight-line distance; a shorter flood never reached a flag on the far side of a long detour and stranded the march on the beach.
  - **Concurrent manual attacks**: a player can have any number of manual attacks under way at once, limited only by manpower. The server locks each ATTACK separately and reserves its cost on the funding flag (`musterReservedByKey`), so one flag can fund several attacks while its staged amount covers them all. The client mirrors this: marches are tracked per attack (`musterTransitByCommand`, keyed by commandId) and a flag can fund a new attack from whatever its unresolved attacks haven't reserved (`musterAvailableAt`, `client-muster-transit.ts`). The client still sends one frontier command at a time, but it releases that slot as soon as the server locks an ATTACK (COMBAT_START) and tracks the fight in `detachedAttacksByCommandId` / `outgoingMusterAttacksByTile` until its COMBAT_RESULT (`client-detached-attacks.ts`, `client-combat-result-slot.ts`). There is no "Capturing Territory" countdown banner; each attack's progress shows on the map and in its tile menu (the new HUD's battle screen and tracker read the same tracking via `ownFightAt` / `latestOwnFight`). A queued tile that can only be reached through a tile still being fought over waits for that fight. Esc does not cancel an attack once the server has locked it: the server's CANCEL_CAPTURE drops every lock the player holds, so Esc only cancels unsent marches and the attack still awaiting the server's acceptance.
  - **Flag capacity fits the commitment**: a flag stages at most `musterFlagCap(manpowerCap, capLevel)` (the larger of 150 and 10% of the manpower cap, +10% per Expand Capacity press, clamped to the manpower cap). The commit slider (March To, March Attack, Launch Attack) goes up to the whole manpower cap, so a commitment above the flag's cap raises `capLevel` to the smallest level that holds it (`musterCapLevelToFit`, `packages/shared/src/muster-config.ts`): SET_MUSTER does it for a flag's own `commitManpower`; a manual attack parked on a flag sends `UPGRADE_MUSTER_CAP` with `fitManpower` (`fitPendingMusterFlagCapacity`, `client-muster-attack-gate.ts`), since its ATTACK only goes out once the flag has filled. Capacity is only ever raised this way, and SET_MUSTER keeps a flag's `capLevel` across mode changes. Each of the player's own flags carries a percentage badge in both renderers (`client-muster-flag-badge/`): toward a parked manual attack's requirement, else a MARCH/ADVANCE flag's `commitManpower` (brass ring, held below 100% until a real delta), else how full the flag is against its cap (verdigris ring). A parked attack can be cancelled with Esc or the flag's Cancel Queued Attack action.
  - **March Attack on a deep enemy tile**: when a hostile tile has no bordering origin (Launch Attack greyed out with "No bordering origin tile or linked dock"), its menu swaps that row for "March Attack from Flag (x, y)" (up to 3 of the player's unbesieged flags with the shortest routes to the target) and "March Attack from New Flag" (the owned land tile with the shortest route, offered while under the flag cap). Routes are measured like the server's march search (`client-march-route.ts`): steps over land, dock links and the player's live aether bridges, with unexplored tiles counted as passable, capped at `MUSTER_MARCH_MAX_ROUTE_STEPS`; every origin must also be within the 30-tile straight-line cap. A tile across open water with no crossing is never offered. A fogged enemy tile (whose menu has no attack row) gets the same rows; when no march is possible the disabled Launch Attack row says why (no flag or owned tile in range, or flags full). The old "Expand To & Attack" waypoint chain for enemy tiles is gone; already-queued waypoints still run. No rows are offered against an empire under truce, since a march never attacks one. Each row carries its origin as `originKey` and dispatches only that origin, and each opens the March To commit sheet from that origin; for a new flag its SET_MUSTER MARCH creates the flag directly in MARCH mode (`client-march-attack-actions/`).
  - **March To on an unexplored tile**: tapping an unexplored tile that doesn't touch the player's land opens a menu with a single "March To" button instead of Expand To -- the player wants to explore that way, not claim that exact tile. Pressing it closes the menu and shows a banner asking where to march from: a button for each nearby muster flag that can reach the tile (only when there is one; the same origins March Attack would offer, `marchAttackOptions`), and, while under the flag cap, an instruction to tap a tile of the player's land to raise a new flag there. While picking, an arrow runs from the hovered tile of the player's land to the destination in both renderers (`state.arrowGesture`). A tapped flag or tile opens the March To commit sheet. A tap on a tile that can't march there (not the player's land, under siege, past the 30-tile cap, no route over land, or flags full for a new flag) says why in the feed and keeps picking. Cancel stops it (`client-march-origin-picking/`). While mustering is still locked (no enemy met yet, `isMusterUnlocked`), the menu falls back to Expand To. Once it is unlocked and nothing can march there, the button is disabled with the reason. Cancel March stays on the marching flag's own tile: the unexplored menu has no tile data for the muster handlers to act on. An unexplored tile touching the player's land is still claimed with a single tap.
  - **Unexplored march targets**: a MARCH target may be an unexplored tile -- `handleMusterMarchTargetClick` arms it (with a feed note that the target is unknown) and the confirm sheet's SET_MUSTER carries only its coordinates; the server routes against the real map, so a flag can be sent into the unknown and reveal it as it expands. The server accepts any terrain, since the player can't know an unexplored tile is a mountain, sea or a cut-off isle. A march ends when the target is captured, or stops short: when the target is not LAND or the march's terrain flood never connects it to the flag (an isle with no dock or bridge crossing, or a land route longer than the flood's cap, which also covers far targets on flags set before the march cap), no unowned land on the flag's side is any nearer, and none of the flag's own steps are still in flight, the flag quietly falls back to HOLD at the closest point with no error (`runtime-muster-march.ts`). The check runs each search, so a crossing built later does not revive a march that already stopped. A route blocked by allied land or a passing combat lock does not stop it; the flag waits ("no target in range") until it is cancelled.
  - **Muster search cost**: MARCH ranks routes with a flood from the target (`muster-march-pathfinding.ts`) that stops once it reaches the flag; this runs every tick a flag is not on cooldown, so keep it bounded and allocation-light (a short march is ~1ms, a 15-tile one ~3ms). A march with a d-step route floods about (2d+1)² tiles, and a flag with no route at all floods the full ~62-step cap (~15.6k tiles). The route plan (field plus traced path, `muster-march-route-plan.ts`) is cached per flag, target and crossing set for 10s (at most 128 plans per world, expired plans swept on insert), so the flood runs about once per 10s per march rather than every tick; a cached plan whose path crosses a tile that is no longer land (e.g. a raised mountain) is re-planned at once.
  - **March route band**: from the distance field the march traces one straightest path to its target (`muster-march-path-band.ts`) and expands or fights only within 1 tile of it (a 3-wide spear). It walks its own land within the 10-hop disc ADVANCE uses plus, beyond it, a strip 2 tiles either side of the path, up to `MUSTER_MARCH_MAX_ROUTE_STEPS` hops; either way only tiles at most `MARCH_ROUTE_CORRIDOR_SLACK` (3) steps longer than the shortest route. The path never steps onto water or mountains. Travel time for its automated attacks is still capped at `MUSTER_TRANSIT_MAX_TILES` (15) tiles of marching, separate from the march range (see Troop transports below). Previously a route longer than 10 hops (e.g. around a mountain range) stalled the front and the flag spread over every tile closer than itself. Tiles off the band are used only when nothing on it can be taken and the flag has nothing in flight (MARCH only: an ADVANCE flag approaching a hostile through the same search is not held back).
  - **Troop transports (travel time across water)**: a muster company's travel time to the tile it launches from (ADVANCE/MARCH automated attacks and claims, and a manual attack funded by a flag) follows its real route (`planMusterTransitRoute`, `packages/shared/src/muster-transit-route/`): a straight walk over land is used as is; where it would cross water or mountains, a quickest-route search over land (each crossing costed at its sail time, the player's aether bridges crossed on foot as one step, budget `MUSTER_MARCH_MAX_ROUTE_STEPS` tiles of marching plus one full crossing) sends it round the obstacle or to one of the player's own docks, across in a troop transport (same landing ground as dock attacks) and on from the landing. Marching costs `MUSTER_TRANSIT_MS_PER_TILE` per tile (floored at 1, capped at `MUSTER_TRANSIT_MAX_TILES`); each crossing adds `MUSTER_SAIL_MS_PER_TILE` (750 ms) per tile between the two ends, at least `MUSTER_SAIL_MIN_MS` (4 s), with all crossings together capped at `MUSTER_SAIL_MAX_MS` (30 s). With no route in reach the old straight-line time is used. The search runs per automated fire, not per tick, and only when the straight walk is blocked; it is uncached and stops when it reaches the launch tile. The simulation applies it in `apps/simulation/src/muster-transit-delay/`; the client plans the same route from its own tiles and dock pairs (`client-muster-transit-route/`; unseen tiles count as land and bridges aren't modelled, so under fog its drawn route can differ, while the server's time stays authoritative), memoised for 5 s, and spreads the server's `transitEndsAt` over the legs: 3D draws the company walking each land leg and a procedural transport steaming the sea lane (`client-map-3d-muster-boat-overlay.ts`), 2D routes the march line through the dock with a boat glyph during the crossing (`client-muster-route-2d/`).
  - **Picking and moving a march target (client)**: while March To is armed a banner says which flag is marching and to tap the destination (with distance under the pointer and a Cancel), and an arrow follows the pointer from the flag. A marching flag offers **Change Target…**, and the march's destination can be dragged on the map to a new tile (after 12px of pointer travel, so a shaky tap never moves it), which re-sends SET_MUSTER MARCH without the confirm sheet (the server keeps the flag's commitment); a besieged flag is refused with the siege reason, as Change Target is. Both renderers draw the preview through `state.arrowGesture` (`client-march-interaction/`).
  - **Flags on town tiles (HUD v2)**: tapping a town or support tile that carries your muster flag selects the flag (normal tile bar, no close-up); tapping it again opens the town screen. The pending first tap is forgotten once the bar is closed by anything but a press on the map (✕, Esc, the HUD), or when a tap lands elsewhere, so the next tap selects the flag again. Re-tapping the open bar's own tile still counts as the second tap, however long the press: the dock reports which map press hid the menu and which press the following tap ended (`client-hud-v2-flag-tap.ts`). On the town screen, Raise flag targets the selected tile (or the town when that tile cannot take one), so a support tile can carry its own flag; the town's own flag controls stay available, marked "(town)".
  - **Marching on a town or structure**: when the target is an enemy (non-allied, non-truced) town or structure tile, the march first takes tiles touching the target until `MARCH_SIEGE_ATTACK_VECTORS` (2) of its neighbours are owned (fewer when no other neighbour can be taken), then attacks only the target, saving up manpower for it (status: not enough manpower) instead of expanding around it; a target needing more than the flag can ever hold is reported as `unfundableTarget` so the client points at Expand Capacity (`muster-march-siege.ts`).
  - **Combat economics**: barbarians bypass coin and manpower gates (`runtime.ts:1263, 2793`) and are treated as a system actor by the planner.
- **Legacy constants still present, mostly unused by the rewrite**: `BARBARIAN_OWNER_ID`, `BARBARIAN_TICK_MS`, `MIN_ACTIVE_BARBARIAN_AGENTS`, `BARBARIAN_MAINTENANCE_INTERVAL_MS`, `BARBARIAN_MAINTENANCE_MAX_SPAWNS_PER_PASS` (`packages/game-domain/src/server-game-constants/server-game-constants.ts:9-37`) and the `BarbarianAgent` type (`packages/shared/src/types.ts:458-465`) are legacy-flavored. Treat them as stale unless you find an active call site.

## 3. Resources and economy

For detailed live rules and a code-owner map for manpower, resource slots,
coin, and dormancy, use [`product/resource-and-manpower-economy.md`](product/resource-and-manpower-economy.md).

- **Developed resource networks**: two frontier deposits remain (neural resin, conductive minerals and catalyst were cut; recovery drops their saved deposits and installations). Luxury biota supplies 2 slots per site, and each slot lets a City, Great City or Metropolis give one more tier of manpower (oldest-held towns first; with none it gives Town manpower); precursor salvage gives +1% attack and defense per site (max +10%) and holds 3 loads that can be shipped to the Court for an Imperial delivery of 25% of the manpower cap. No frontier resource pays Coin or needs a recipient building. Standard maps target 90 tiles of salvage, plus 1.5 biota tiles per town (about 550 on a 360-town map), in 3–6 tile deposits in each type's preferred habitat only. See the [economy reference](product/resource-and-manpower-economy.md#frontier-resource-networks). Reclamation Works shares the Manufactory unlock.
- **Waystations** grant one reward on first capture, then disappear from the map. When an AFC lands at runtime (new join, recovery or respawn), unclaimed waystations in the part of its reach disk the landing will auto-claim (town reach radius including bonuses; not tiles in a rival's reach or mid-fight) are removed instead of granted, so the landing never pays one out. Seeded world-init spawns need no such clear: their reach is seeded without a neutral auto-claim (`apps/simulation/src/afc-landing-footprint/afc-landing-waystation-clear.ts`). A resource reward turns that same tile into a Farm, Titanium, Crystal, or Umbrite site using the existing lowest-resource-balance rule. The site follows normal garrison and resource rules. A TECH reward grants through `grantFreeTech` (`apps/simulation/src/tech-grant-side-effects/`), the same path as paid research, so a granted AFC module such as the Titanium Forge Module is installed on the player's AFC. `apps/simulation/src/runtime-waystation-activation.ts`
- **Resource model**: tile resource kinds and player-economy resources are distinct. FOOD, TITANIUM, CRYSTAL, and UMBRITE power global resource-slot pools rather than being stockpiled currencies. See the focused economy reference for supply, demand, and converter rules. `packages/shared/src/structure-slots/structure-slots.ts`
- **Coin and support**: coin is passive per-minute income and is rescaled by `GOLD_RESCALE_DIVISOR = 288`; town/network and structure modifiers apply in the simulation economy module. An unfed town produces no coin until its separate support system recovers. Coin above a town-linked cap is lost. `packages/game-domain/src/server-game-constants/server-game-constants.ts`, `apps/simulation/src/player-update-economy/`
- **Manpower**: cap and regeneration come from the starting capital, towns, terrain, and qualifying structures/networks. Regeneration is credited in one refill every 4 hours (staggered per player), not continuously. The current tier values, diminishing town weighting, and action costs are in the focused economy reference and `packages/shared/src/config.ts`.
- **Slots and dormancy**: structures occupy resource slots from build start through removal. A shortfall automatically makes the newest relevant consumers dormant first; this is not periodic resource drain. `apps/simulation/src/resource-slot-view/resource-slot-view.ts`

## 4. Units (intentionally absent)

There are no unit pieces. Combat is **tile-ownership transitions**:

- **ATTACK**: origin is owned by attacker, target is owned by an enemy. Manpower cost varies (`ATTACK_MANPOWER_COST`-family constants, modified by fort presence and breach-shock state). Combat resolves after `COMBAT_LOCK_MS` (15s, halved from 30s; Steam Vanguard's −2.5s and Assault Command Lattice's −5s stack on top, floored at 5s). Because barbarian tiles rest `BARBARIAN_TILE_REST_MS` (15s) after each fight resolves, a barbarian tile's attack cycle is 30s. Winner takes the tile.
- **Terrain combat**: a defending tile on hills (`isHillsTileAt`) defends at `HILLS_DEFENSE_MULT` (1.2x), and an attack whose origin and target are separated by a river edge attacks at `RIVER_CROSSING_ATTACK_MULT` (0.8x); both shown as "Hills" / "River crossing" rows in the odds breakdown. Hills only matter where the tile defends at all (SETTLED, or FRONTIER inside its capture hold); undefended frontier is still a free capture. A diagonal attack crosses when a river edge touches the shared corner and neither L-shaped route around it is a dry land way round (a route either crosses a river edge or passes a worldgen sea/mountain tile, as at a river mouth or headwater); dock crossings, aether bridges and pre-v9 (centre-river) worlds never count as river crossings. Forest gives no combat term. Both read procedural worldgen terrain only, so no state is stored. The simulation derives them itself (`resolveAttackCombat`); the gateway can't build terrain (about 12s per season), so its attack preview takes the `hillsDefense`/`riverCrossing` facts from the client's ATTACK_PREVIEW, computed on the client's own seeded worldgen, and ignores a river fact on a March To preview: the flag's final launch tile isn't known yet, so a march can roll lower than its preview when that hop crosses a river. A client that sends no facts (an older bundle) gets a preview without either term. The client's march-line estimates apply the hills term only. Client origin selection (`pickOriginForTarget`) ranks each neighbour at its river-penalised attack, so a same-bank origin wins a tie; the simulation's muster/advance launch-tile picks and the AI planner do not model terrain. Applies to running seasons from deploy (no worldgen-version gate); pre-v9 seasons have no edge rivers, so only hills apply there. `packages/shared/src/terrain-combat/terrain-combat.ts`, `packages/shared/src/frontier-combat/frontier-combat.ts`, `apps/simulation/src/runtime-combat-support.ts`, `apps/realtime-gateway/src/attack-preview/attack-preview.ts`
- **Capture hold**: FRONTIER ground normally has zero defense, so an ATTACK on it is a guaranteed capture with no roll. A tile won by ATTACK from another player (not from barbarians, not by EXPAND) instead gets `captureHoldUntil = now + CAPTURE_HOLD_MS` (60s): while it is still FRONTIER and inside that window it defends at `CAPTURE_HOLD_DEFENSE_MULT` (1.0x), with defender infrastructure/tech and breach shock still applying but no garrison, town or fort bonus (forts only defend once SETTLED). Attacks on it roll real combat and emit a battle broadcast; the rolled result carries `defendedFrontier` so a fight launched inside the window still plays as a battle if the window ends before it resolves. The muster cost stays the FRONTIER cost (`FRONTIER_ATTACK_MUSTER_COST`), and that is also the floor effort scales against: committing more (Extra/Double) multiplies the odds ratio by commit/floor and the attacker loses exactly the commitment, win or lose, as on SETTLED targets (`isCommitOddsEligibleTarget`). A muster flag's commitment is chosen against `MUSTER_ATTACK_COST`, so its attacks on a held tile send it scaled to the frontier floor (`flagCommitForTarget`, keeping the effort ratio). Shield flags do not cover held tiles. The hold is saved in snapshots and survives a restart; `replaceTileState` drops it once it expires, the tile leaves FRONTIER, or a new owner would merely inherit it (`runtime-tile-write-rules.ts`). The AI planner only gives its free-capture bonus to undefended frontier, not to a held tile. Presentation: in 3D the capturing squad keeps standing on the tile for the window and is the defending squad in any fight there; in 2D a rank of owner-coloured figures with a time-left bar marks the tile; the tile menu shows a countdown. **Capture aftermath**: a held tile can't finish garrisoning before its hold ends -- `startSettlementProcess` floors the garrison time at the hold's remaining time (`captureHoldSettleFloorMs`), whatever settle-speed effects apply, and nothing else can settle a held tile early: RUSH_BUY of its garrison is rejected, a sealed pocket's auto-fill leaves it FRONTIER, and a replacement AFC can't land on it (`apps/simulation/src/capture-hold-garrison/`) -- so a captured fort gives no defense and a captured resource pays no resource slots (both only count once SETTLED) until then. While held, a captured fort also grants none of its combat terms (fort-tier floor, "vs forts" attack, Citadel/ward), in the sim's `targetHasActiveFortFor` and the gateway attack preview alike. On capture, Relay Beacons and Siege Batteries are razed, as are any structures still under construction, a SETTLEMENT-tier town and any muster flag on the tile; other built structures (forts, Aether Towers, economic structures, AFCs) transfer (`capture-structures.ts`). Aether Purge applies the same razing to the tile it neutralises (`abandonedStructureFields`): beacons, siege engines and anything under construction are destroyed (an unfinished fort upgrade falls back to its standing tier), other built structures stay on the neutral tile for whoever claims it next (`runtime-aether-lance-command-handler.ts`). **Razed wreck**: a razed (finished) Relay Beacon, Siege Battery, Siege Tower or Dread Tower lies wrecked where it stood for `RAZED_WRECK_DURATION_MS` (24h), presentation only: any capture (including the lost-origin revert) or Aether Purge stamps `razedWreck`/`razedWreckUntil` on the tile (`razedWreckFieldsOnCapture`, `packages/shared/src/razed-wreck/razed-wreck.ts`), a later capture keeps an existing wreck's clock, and `replaceTileState` drops the fields once cleared (`withoutClearedRazedWreck`). Saved in snapshots and sent on the wire (`razed_wreck`/`razed_wreck_until`), so it survives reloads and every player sees it; the renderers stop drawing it once anything is built on the tile. Presentation, both renderers, until the hold ends: a captured fort stands breached, one wall smashed into two stubs with its stone spilled through the gap and a corner tower snapped with its top fallen inside; a captured resource burns; a captured dock is set ablaze on its own quay (harbour house, freight, coal, pier and moored ferry), and pays no gold until the hold ends and it is garrisoned -- dock income is SETTLED-only and the garrison floor above holds it FRONTIER until then (`player-update-economy-captured-dock.test.ts`). In 3D the wrecks are modelled from the structures' own parts and drawn with the building materials -- embers, sputtering aether, soot smoke on the steam layer, soft char decals (`client-wreck-models/`, `client-map-3d-wreck-overlay.ts`); the fort breach reuses the fort overlay's own wall/tower meshes (`client-map-3d-fort-breach.ts`). In 2D the same pieces are painted (`client-map-2d-capture-aftermath.ts`, the breach over the fort sprite). The tile menu explains each (`packages/client/src/client-capture-aftermath/`, `client-map-3d-capture-aftermath-fx.ts`). `packages/shared/src/capture-hold/capture-hold.ts`, `apps/simulation/src/runtime-lock-resolution.ts`, `apps/simulation/src/runtime-combat-support.ts`, `packages/client/src/client-map-3d-capture-hold-garrison.ts`, `packages/client/src/client-map-2d-capture-hold-overlay.ts`
- **Battle scars** (presentation only, no rule effect): when a garrisoned (SETTLED) tile is captured in battle from another player (not from barbarians), the ground keeps signs of war -- `warScarLevel` (1..`WAR_SCAR_MAX_LEVEL`, 5) and `warScarUntil` (`WAR_SCAR_DURATION_MS`, 24h after the latest battle). Every later battle on a scarred tile -- another capture, a held-frontier fight, or a repelled attack -- adds a level and restarts the 24h, so contested ground looks worse the more it is fought over; an unscarred tile only gets scars from a captured garrison. Scars belong to the ground, so they stay through later changes of owner, and `replaceTileState` drops them once faded (`withoutFadedWarScars`). Saved in snapshots and sent on the wire (`war_scar_level`/`war_scar_until`, so every player sees the same scars after a reload). The damage is beam-weapon damage only: furrows cut and fused to dark glass and glassed pits in rings of melt, on charred earth, more of each per level (1 cut/1 pit up to 6 cuts/5 pits, crossfire from level 3) -- no thrown earth or wreckage. For `WAR_SCAR_FRESH_MS` (2h) after the latest battle the scars are fresh (`isWarScarFresh`): the cuts glow molten and the ground smokes; after that they are cold. In a forest the beams burn the trees instead: each tree in the tile's layout gets a fate (standing, charred where it stands, sliced through with its top down, burnt to a stump), more burnt with each level (about 40% at 1, all at 5), a burnt tree staying burnt; the forest draws only the standing ones and the burnt ones are drawn in their places in their forest's own way (conifer spars, bare broadleaves, black palm poles), smouldering and smoking while fresh, in both renderers (`client-forest-war/`). 3D: `client-wreck-models/beam-scars.ts`, `battle-scar-models.ts` (three variants per level), `forest-war-model.ts`; 2D: `client-map-2d-war-scars.ts`; the tile menu shows the level and hours left. `packages/shared/src/war-scars/war-scars.ts`, `apps/simulation/src/runtime-winning-capture.ts`, `apps/simulation/src/runtime-lock-resolution.ts`
- **EXPAND**: origin owned by attacker, target is neutral. Ownership transitions after `FRONTIER_CLAIM_MS`. The manpower price scales with the FRONTIER tiles the player holds right now (garrisoning lowers it): 10 below 100, then 11 at 100, 12 at 150, 13 at 175, 15 at 200, 50 at 1,000 and 100 at 2,000, interpolated and floored between anchors and continuing at 1 per 20 tiles past 2,000 (`expandManpowerCostForFrontierCount`, `packages/shared/src/expand-manpower-cost/expand-manpower-cost.ts`). Town auto-claims (the involuntary ring around a settled town) and barbarians keep the flat 10; EXPAND rush-buy pricing still uses the base 10. The sim sends the current price as `expandManpowerCost` on `PLAYER_UPDATE` for the client's menus and pre-checks.
- **SETTLE**: (shown to players as **Garrison**; settled land is shown as "garrisoned land" -- internal names stay SETTLE/SETTLED) target must ALREADY be owned by the caller and `ownershipState === "FRONTIER"` (i.e. previously claimed via EXPAND, not neutral) — rejected `SETTLE_INVALID` otherwise. Costs `SETTLE_MANPOWER_COST` (20) + coin, resolves after a timer, then flips the tile to `ownershipState: "SETTLED"`. Critically, it does **not** fabricate a town: `resolvePendingSettlement` only carries a `town` record forward if the tile already had one (`...(latest.town ? { town: latest.town } : {})`, `apps/simulation/src/runtime/runtime.ts` in `resolvePendingSettlement`) — settling bare frontier land with no pre-existing town produces plain SETTLED land, not a town. `packages/shared/src/config.ts:92`
- **Garrison duration**: there is no separate garrison timer -- a garrison takes exactly as long as an EXPAND on the same tile (`garrisonBaseDurationMsAt` = `frontierClaimDurationMsAt`: `FRONTIER_CLAIM_MS` 7.5s, 1.5x on forest and hills), divided by the player's `settlementSpeedMult` effects. A player's first `INSTANT_GARRISON_COUNT` (10) lifetime garrisons take 0 ms. `startSettlementProcess` counts every garrison it starts on `DomainPlayer.lifetimeGarrisons`, which is saved with the player in snapshots, replayed from `SETTLEMENT_STARTED` on event recovery, and never reset on respawn (respawn reuses the player record); a record saved before the counter existed reads as 0. Every garrison started counts, including ones auto-garrison places, garrisons of a captured tile still under its hold (where the free one gives no speedup) and ones later cancelled. The capture-hold floor still applies, so even an instant garrison of a held tile waits for the hold to end. PLAYER_UPDATE sends `instantGarrisonsRemaining` so the client predicts the same time and the tile menu shows "Instant (N free left)". `packages/shared/src/garrison-duration/garrison-duration.ts`, `apps/simulation/src/runtime/runtime-settlement-start.ts`
- **Auto-garrison opt-in**: each player has one switch for towns/docks (plus the plain tiles of a grown town's support ring) and one per resource (FARM, FISH, TITANIUM, UMBRITE, GEMS and each network resource), set from Settings or the tile menu's Garrison checkbox via `SET_AUTO_SETTLE_PREFS`. Auto-garrison only spends manpower on FRONTIER tiles whose switch is on. New humans start with everything off until they answer; AI and legacy players have everything on. Prefs saved under the older food/strategic/luxury (or single `resources`) switches apply to every resource in that group. Persisted and sent prefs also carry derived food/strategic/luxury switches (on only when every resource in the group is on; `autoSettlePrefsForOutput`), so a rolled-back sim or a tab on the previous bundle reads an opt-out as an opt-out. `packages/shared/src/auto-settle-prefs/auto-settle-prefs.ts`, `apps/simulation/src/runtime-auto-settle-prefs-command-handler.ts`
- **Auto-improve opt-in**: a second set of per-resource switches, stored with the auto-garrison prefs (`improve`, all off by default, AI included). Each territory-automation tick (30s), after auto-garrison, the sim builds the resource's improvement (FARM: Hydrogarden; TITANIUM/GEMS: Mine; UMBRITE: Umbrite Rig; each network resource: its network building; FISH has none) on up to 3 of the player's garrisoned, unbuilt tiles of a switched-on resource. It goes through the normal BUILD_STRUCTURE handler, so cost, tech, AFC, resource-slot and development-slot rules are unchanged. Resource slots held by the player's own queued builds are left alone. A tile that can't be built yet is skipped and retried on the next tick, and no rejection is sent. A shortage of manpower or slots holds back every tile of that structure type for the tick; a one-tile blocker such as an AFC or relic footprint skips only that tile. Town and dock tiles are left to the town build rules. A tile the handler refuses anyway is dropped until it next changes. A structure removed or destroyed on a tile the owner still holds is not rebuilt (the tile is not re-added to `improvableTilesByOwner`, which is rebuilt at boot, so a restart makes it a candidate again). Candidates come from the per-owner `improvableTilesByOwner` index, so the tick never scans the map. Set from Settings or the checkbox under a resource improvement's Place button (`SET_AUTO_SETTLE_PREFS` with `improve`; only the switches it names change). `apps/simulation/src/runtime-auto-improve/`, `packages/shared/src/auto-settle-prefs/auto-improve-prefs.ts`
- **Garrison Many (client)**: once a settled town and food tile are known, the frontier menu opens an editable selection (up to 60 tiles). Tap tiles to select/deselect; pan and pinch keep working. Both 2D and 3D show selections in gold and invalidated tiles in orange. The bottom bar shows selected count, total cost, Confirm and Cancel. Confirm revalidates reach (including normal settlement exemptions), ownership, existing garrisons, queue capacity and cumulative affordability including queued garrisons. An unavailable selected tile blocks the batch until deselected; valid selections enter the existing paced development queue directly, paying as each starts. There is no separate review screen or queue breakdown. Cancel/Escape or disconnection clears this temporary selection.
- **Queued garrisons wait for manpower**: a queued build, or a Garrison the pool can cover, holds its manpower on the server from the moment it is queued (`runtime-dev-queue-build-reservation.ts`). A Garrison queued while the pool is short of `SETTLE_MANPOWER_COST` is accepted anyway as `awaitingManpower`, holding nothing until a refill batch covers it: each 15 s passive-income tick settles manpower and reserves waiting garrisons in queue order (whether or not a development slot is free, the same as a build's hold), then tries a drain (`runtime-dev-queue-settle-manpower-wait.ts`). A held garrison's manpower goes back unclamped right before its own SETTLE charges it, so regen refilling the pool to the cap during the hold can't make it cost twice. The server drain skips any garrison it can't pay for rather than dispatching it to be refused. A player-sent SETTLE first releases its own queue entry's reservation (`releaseQueuedSettleReservation`), since the client's `DEV_QUEUE_CANCEL` rides a slower lane. On the client, tapping Garrison without enough manpower queues the garrison instead of refusing it. `processDevelopmentQueue` leaves a garrison the pool can't cover in place and sends the first entry behind it that can go; PLAYER_UPDATE's per-entry `manpowerReserved` flag lets it send a reserved garrison without re-checking the local pool (`client-development-dispatch.ts`). The tile menu's queue card reads "Needs manpower" while a garrison waits.

- **Combat locks and muster sieges**: a fight locks only its *target* tile until it resolves; the tile it launched from stays free (the defender may attack it back). A tile that is the target of an enemy fight can't launch an attack -- the server answers `LOCKED` with "launch tile is under attack", while a target already being fought over answers `LOCKED` with "tile locked in combat" (`packages/shared/src/combat-lock-messages/`). A muster flag on such a tile is **under siege**: it can't fund any attack (`resolveMusterSource` skips it), be set to ADVANCE/MARCH, or be cleared (`MUSTER_UNDER_SIEGE`, `apps/simulation/src/muster-siege-guard/`); HOLD, Cancel March and Expand Capacity still work. The client greys out those flag actions with a countdown, holds a queued attack that would launch from a besieged flag or tile until the fight resolves (`client-queue-muster-dispatch.ts`), prefers a free neighbouring launch tile (`client-origin-selection.ts`), and marks the flag with a pulsing red ring and guarding soldiers in 3D and a ringed pennant badge in 2D (`client-muster-siege/`).
- **Attacks freeze development on the target tile.** While an ATTACK lock targets a tile, a pending SETTLE on it is cancelled and refunded when the lock is accepted, a new SETTLE/BUILD on it is rejected ("tile is under attack"), and any `under_construction` structure is *paused*: `pausedAt` is stamped on the structure, its completion timer becomes a no-op, and rush-buy is refused. When the lock releases (defender held, attack cancelled, or lock swept) the structure's `completesAt` slides forward by the time spent paused and a fresh timer is armed; if the attacker captures the tile the unfinished build is dropped as before. Logic in `apps/simulation/src/attack-development-hold/attack-development-hold.ts`, triggered from `runtime-frontier-command.ts` (hold) and `CombatLockIndex.onAttackTargetReleased` (resume).
- **Nothing "builds" a town or settlement, and no player command creates one from scratch.** The only ways to *end up owning* a town are: (1) EXPAND then SETTLE onto a tile that already carries a `town` record (almost always one of the neutral towns world gen pre-placed — see §2), or (2) ATTACK an enemy-owned town tile, which likewise transfers the existing town record rather than creating a new one. The one town every player has without doing either is their single free starting SETTLEMENT-tile, and even that is assigned by the *system's* spawn/respawn code, not a player action (`apps/simulation/src/runtime-respawn-helpers.ts:168`). Once owned, a town can *grow* through population tiers over time (SETTLEMENT → TOWN → CITY → GREAT_CITY → METROPOLIS, `packages/shared/src/town-growth/town-growth.ts`) based on food/resources/upkeep — passive growth, not a build action. `packages/shared/src/structure-registry/structure-registry.ts` is the actual buildable-things registry (Relay Beacon, Fort, Dock, etc.) — towns/settlements are never in it.
- Movement is implicit. Frontier actions originate from any adjacent owned tile, or from an owned dock onto the landing ground of a dock it links to, or from aether-bridged tiles. `packages/game-domain/src/index.ts:20, 171-257`, `packages/shared/src/types.ts:415-421`

## 5. Structures

- **One structure per tile** (mutex). Must be placed on a `SETTLED` tile owned by the builder.
- **Categories**:
  - Economic: Hydrogarden, Umbrite Rig, Mine, Incubation Engine, Manufactory, Bank, Synthesizers (Umbrite/Titanium Works/Crystal), Fuel Plant, Trade Nexus, Ore Refinery, Governance (Ministry Hall, Ancillary Depot, Harbor Exchange, Resonance Grid).
  - Military: Fort, Siege Battery, Observatory.
  - Monuments (late-game, ultra-high manpower cost plus a Crystal slot, built in 4 stages): Imperial Exchange, Sovereign Siege Engine, Aegis Dome, Astral Dock.
- **Unlocks**: tech-gated. Costs scale incrementally or exponentially with existing count, in coin + strategic resources.
- **Fort and siege tiers are chosen**: the Fortify and Siege tile-menu rows open a tier picker listing every tier (Palisade, Fort, Titanium Bastion, Thunder Bastion; Siege Battery, Siege Tower, Dread Tower) with its cost, slots and stat, and the reason a tier can't be built (missing research, a module not installed on an active AFC, a slot shortfall, reach). The chosen tier rides `BUILD_FORT`/`BUILD_SIEGE_BATTERY`, `DEV_QUEUE_ENQUEUE` and `CLAIM_CONTINUATION_SET` as an optional `tier`; queued entries persist it. A tier is unlocked when it is no higher than the best tier the tech allows, an upgrade must go above the standing tier but may skip one (it pays the chosen tier's full price, as upgrades always did), and an explicit tier never steps down for slots -- the slot gate reports the shortfall instead. Without a `tier` (the AI, older clients) the build takes the best tier the tech allows, a fresh siege build stepping down to the best tier whose UMBRITE slots fit. Rule: `packages/shared/src/structure-tier-choice/`; menu rows and sheet: `packages/client/src/client-tile-action-logic/client-structure-tier-options.ts`, `packages/client/src/client-structure-tier-sheet/`.
- **Selection (AI)**: `build_economic_structure` scores per tile by:
  1. Resource on tile (FARM → HYDROGARDEN, etc.)
  2. Player need (low food coverage → Hydrogarden/Hydroworks supply or Ministry Hall demand relief; weak economy → income structures)
  3. Adjacency (Ore Refinery's 10-tile output multiplier radius; town support reach)
  4. Town synergy: Manufactory, Clearing House, Trade Nexus, Ancillary Depot/Factory, Reserve Lattice and Neural Works are scored by what they would add given the town's stacked copies and the amplifiers in reach (`apps/simulation/src/ai/build/economic-synergy-scoring.ts`; design in `docs/ai-economic-synergy-plan.md`)
- **Build time** is a flat 60 s for every structure that costs manpower (`STRUCTURE_BUILD_MS`, `structureBuildDurationMs`, `structure-costs.ts`); a 0-manpower structure and the first five Relay Beacons are instant. Manpower, not build time, is the limit on how much a player builds. **Removal** takes a flat 30 s (`STRUCTURE_REMOVAL_MS`, `structureRemovalDurationMs` / `fortRemovalDurationMs` / `siegeBatteryRemovalDurationMs`). Build-speed effects (`fortBuildSpeedMult`, `outpostDeploymentSpeedMult`, `economicStructureBuildSpeedMult`, from techs and the pre-AFC domain tree) divide the build time; which effect applies to which structure is `structureBuildSpeedEffectKey` (`packages/shared/src/structure-registry-index.ts`), shared by the simulation and the client's build menus (`packages/client/src/client-build-speed/`). Removal time ignores them. **AFC manpower discounts** (`fortBuildManpowerCostMult` from Bastion I, `relayBeaconBuildManpowerCostMult` from Dominion I, `structureBuildManpowerCostMult` from Dominion V) multiply the manpower a build charges; `structureManpowerCostEffectKey` picks the per-structure one, and the simulation applies them to the build charge, the development-queue reservation and the cancel refund (`apps/simulation/src/structure-manpower-cost/`), with the client's build menus matching (`packages/client/src/client-structure-manpower-cost/`). In-flight records carry `startedAt` and `completesAt` (stamped by the build/removal handlers, stripped on completion, capture and cancel); the client derives progress from that window.
- **Construction is shown in phases** (presentation only, no gameplay effect): economic structures, Relay Beacons and forts under construction or removal render in 4 height bands (a fort upgrade keeps the standing tier fully drawn) with a parts stack and an ancillary crew (the settle dots) in both the 3D and 2D maps, plus scaffolding and AFC supply pods in 3D. Model: `packages/client/src/client-construction-phase/`; design and coverage in `docs/construction-animation-plan.md`.

## 6. Tech and research

- **Tech tree**: DAG with prerequisites; tier-based. Tree config is per-season (serialized config ID), so tech contents can vary across seasons.
- **Effects**: each tech can unlock structures, grant stat mods (`attack`, `defense`, `income`, `vision` multipliers), or grant ability access.
- **Research**: one tech at a time per player. Completes instantly on purchase (cost in coin only) — no research timer (`researchTimeMult` was removed as a dead effect).
- "Domination income" is a misnomer in earlier docs — there is no income mechanic tied to domination. Town Control is a victory *path*, not an income modifier.
- References: tech tree data lives at `packages/game-domain/data/tech-tree.json`; the bridge that scores tech selection in the AI lives at `apps/simulation/src/tech-domain-bridge/tech-domain-bridge.ts`. Player-stat type: `packages/shared/src/types.ts:389`.

### AFC specializations (new campaigns)

Season marker `afcUpgradeRuleset: "afc-v1"` selects the shared thirty-entry catalogue.

## 8. Diplomacy

- **Truces**: two-player non-aggression pacts. 12h or 24h duration. A pending truce offer waits for the other player, including one who is offline (they get a truce-request email): it only lapses after `TRUCE_REQUEST_TTL_MS` (7 days, a cleanup backstop). Any combat between the two players (`COMBAT_RESOLVED` with one as attacker and the other as defender: manual attacks, muster flags, marches, sieges) withdraws every pending truce offer between them in both directions and tells both players, so an old offer can't be accepted mid-war on peacetime terms (`social-combat-offer-withdrawal.ts`); offers already being paid out finish. A truce offer between empires already at truce is an extension (`extendsTruce` on the request): accepting adds its hours to the current end, and a truce may never last more than 48h in total from when it began, extensions included (`TRUCE_MAX_LIFETIME_MS`, `packages/shared/src/truce-extension/`; an offer that would pass it is refused with `TRUCE_EXTENSION_CAP`), so repeated extensions can't keep one truce running for weeks. This limits extensions only: once a truce ends, the same pair can agree a new truce straight away (no re-truce cooldown). Pending-offer, re-offer cooldown and lockout rules are the same as for a new truce. An offer is priced and worded as an extension or a new truce when it is made, so if the truce lapses, is broken or is imposed by a Court mandate before it is accepted, accepting is refused as stale (before any payment; `apps/realtime-gateway/src/social-state/social-truce-extension.ts`). Extending a Court armistice turns it into an ordinary, breakable truce that a campaign reset no longer clears. Tracked in `SocialActiveTruce` (start/end, creator) in the realtime gateway's social state, not the simulation. Breaking a truce early (`TRUCE_BREAK`) locks the breaker out of requesting or accepting any new truce for `TRUCE_BREAK_LOCKOUT_MS` (24h); the other party is unaffected. Launch Attack (or Attack Connected Region) on a truced empire's tile opens a confirmation listing what breaking costs (lockout, profile record, AI opinion) and sends `TRUCE_BREAK` only if confirmed; under a Court armistice the attack rows are disabled. On an ally's tile, Launch Attack confirms and starts the 24h alliance break notice (`ALLIANCE_BREAK`) instead: nothing is attacked or mustered, and the row stays disabled with the hours left until the break completes (`packages/client/src/client-pact-attack-gate/`, `client-break-pact-sheet/`). Break Truce in the tile menu and the Alliances tab uses the same confirmation. Seasonal AI truce targets exist (recent commit `fee1f72`). `apps/realtime-gateway/src/social-state/social-state.ts`, `packages/game-domain/src/server-game-constants/server-game-constants.ts:26-27`, `packages/shared/src/types.ts:129-147, 50-67`
- **Alliances**: mutual `allies` membership on each player. Frontier validation rejects attacks against allies.
- **War declarations**: implicit — any frontier action against a non-allied, non-truced player is hostile. No formal declaration step.
- **Re-offer cooldown**: after a truce or alliance offer is rejected or cancelled, the sender can't make the same kind of offer to that player for 30 minutes (10 minutes after an unanswered truce offer lapses at the 7-day backstop). Directional and per kind. `apps/realtime-gateway/src/social-state/social-offer-cooldowns.ts`
- **Offer terms**: truce and alliance offers can carry Coin and continuing resource supply each side promises (`DiplomacyTerms`, `packages/shared/src/diplomacy-terms/`). Nothing moves until accept: the gateway locks the offer, the simulation's `SETTLE_DIPLOMACY_TERMS` checks both balances and moves everything at once, then the agreement is recorded (refunded if it can't be). `apps/realtime-gateway/src/diplomacy-settlement/`
- **Resource trades**: five pooled supply resources are negotiable: FOOD, Titanium, Crystal, Umbrite and luxury biota. Precursor salvage is finite and can't be traded or siphoned. The Resource trade option works independently of treaties, including between existing allies; resource terms can also accompany a truce/alliance. Only spare domestic output can be newly offered; imports do not become export capacity. On acceptance, slots transfer exclusively until either House ends the supply agreement. Frontier supply supports the recipient's industries. Coin settles once and is not refunded when supply ends. Resource deliveries in both directions pause if either promise lacks backing and resume when output recovers. Cancellation releases both directions after settling their existing income at the old rate. Breaking or expiring the accompanying treaty does not automatically cancel the supply contract, but war does: when one House's ATTACK on another House's tile (settled or frontier, launched by hand or from a muster flag) is accepted, every supply agreement between the two ends in both directions through the ordinary cancellation path, and their pending independent offers are withdrawn. Attacks by or on barbarians touch no trades. Launching an attack (single, bulk or connected region) on a House you trade with first asks to confirm that it ends those trades, and so do March To (or dragging a march's destination) onto a trade partner's tile, and Set Advance on a flag whose reach (own or neutral land, up to the advance range, one of your docks crossing to its linked docks' landing ground; Aether bridges not counted) holds a visible, non-allied, untruced trade partner's FRONTIER/SETTLED tile (`packages/client/src/client-flag-supply-trade-confirm/`). These are client checks at order time: a march whose route cuts through a partner's land, or an Advance flag that later reaches a partner (new trade, partner expands), still ends the trades without asking; the attack row and the March To sheet carry the same warning, and the Set Advance sheet notes it whenever any trade is running. Only ATTACK counts: Aether abilities, siphons and bombardments leave trades running. An AI never asks a House it fought in the last 6h for FOOD. Pending independent offers expire after 24h; accepted supply has no expiry. Uncertain payments retain a durable offer and stable command identity so retrying cannot pay twice. `apps/simulation/src/resource-supply-trades/`, `apps/realtime-gateway/src/resource-trade/`

- **Counter-offers**: "Counter" on an incoming truce or alliance offer opens the trade screen pre-filled with that offer from the receiver's side; any kind, duration or terms can be sent back. The request carries `counters: { kind, requestId }` and the gateway rejects the countered offer in the same step, without a re-offer cooldown (`apps/realtime-gateway/src/diplomacy-counter-offer/`). If the counter itself is then refused (cooldown, lockout), the original offer is still gone.
- **Diplomacy notifications**: incoming truce and alliance offers wait in the client's offer dock (`packages/client/src/client-diplomacy-offer-dock/`) with their terms, a countdown (on the server's clock, `serverNow` on INIT and TRUCE_UPDATE) and Accept / Decline, instead of an on-map popup; Accept on an offer that costs the recipient coin needs a second tap, and an offer can be hidden to answer later in Alliances. Truces starting (an extension adds no row), ending (including natural expiry, which the gateway's maintenance sweep now pushes to both players) and breaking, and alliances forming and fully breaking, are listed in the Chronicle / Activity "Yours" timeline as `DIPLOMACY` cards that the gateway merges into the simulation's timeline (`apps/realtime-gateway/src/social-state/social-diplomacy-history.ts`, persisted in the social store's `social_diplomacy_history` table, last 24h, 20 rows per player; finished alliance breaks come from `social_completed_alliance_breaks`) and in the Activity feed. Only the victim of a broken truce still gets a popup (told apart from the breaker by the breaker's new `truceBreaksThisSeason` record, not the announcement text).
- **AI diplomacy (Civ-style)**: each AI keeps a fading memory of each player (tiles taken either way, fights, last attack, truces bought, coin paid; `ai-diplomacy-memory.ts`, fed from COMBAT_RESOLVED). Attitude (Furious..Friendly) is a sum of named modifiers shown on the trade screen (`ai-diplomacy-opinion.ts`). Broken pacts: the betrayed AI holds −60 all season for a broken truce; every other AI holds −10 per truce broken in the last 7 days (max −30); an AI whose alliance the player started breaking holds −10 for 7 days from the notice (`BROKEN_*` constants in `packages/shared/src/diplomacy-betrayal/`, windows read in `ai-diplomacy-grudges.ts`). An extension is priced like a new truce of that length (each accepted extension counts as a truce bought, so the +50% repeat surcharge builds up), except the AI never pays for one (`extensionPricing`). Truce and alliance prices are hours of the AI's own income, floored at 10 coin a day; the buyer's income doesn't count. A truce costs: free if borders don't meet and no fighting, 12h for neighbours, 24h at war, 48h if the AI is winning, +1h per tile taken (reparations), scaled by attitude, +50% per recent truce bought; desperate AIs drop the base price. Within 30 min of the player attacking (fresh aggression) the truce price is +50% and the AI never pays for peace (a losing AI asks reparations for tiles taken instead), unless it is suing for peace (below); it refuses outright only while it has a counter-attack ready (free + mustered manpower, plus a refill due inside the window, at 50%+ of cap) and the border is worth fighting for (no other land to grow into, or it is winning), and says how many minutes remain (`ai-retaliation-readiness.ts`; with no read on its army it refuses as before). AIs don't propose truces to a player who attacked them inside that window, unless suing. Truce expiry: each `SYNC_TRUCE` carries the truce's `endsAt` (re-sent when an extension moves it, persisted with the player and replayed from `SOCIAL_STATE_SYNCED`), and in the last 90 min of any truce an AI raises its war reserve to 60% of cap (`TRUCE_EXPIRY_PREP_MS`, `TRUCE_EXPIRY_RESERVE_CAP_FRACTION` in `automation-command-planner-war-reserve.ts`) so EXPAND/SETTLE/builds stop spending and manpower is banked for when the truce ends; ATTACK stays exempt. Furious AIs refuse unless they are losing the war (never for a player who broke a truce with them). An AI losing a real war (it has lost 2+ more tiles to the player than it took, or is outmatched/desperate and gaining no ground) pays for peace (capped, once per war), and the trade screen says which and shows the war tally (both fading tile counts) it judged by. The AI also reads the next two hours of the war (`ai-war-outlook.ts`) from an hour-scale fading tile count (`WAR_PACE_HALF_LIFE_MS`) and both sides' manpower reloads (refill offsets come from the player id, so it knows the rival's too): losing a tile or more net lately while spent now (less fighting strength than the rival), but reloading within 2h to at least half its cap and at least the rival's strength by then (the rival's reload counted if it lands first), it holds out and isn't counted as losing ("expects to win its land back when its armies reload in N min"); having lost 3+ net tiles lately with less fighting strength than the rival and no such reload, it sues for peace: it counts as losing, pays even inside the fresh-aggression window, pays for the land it expects to lose (an hour of income per projected tile, between 6h and 12h, at most 40% of its coin; `AI_SUING_*`), and proposes that paid 12h truce itself on its proactive tick, as it would otherwise only do outside that window. It doesn't sue a player it paid for peace within the last 24h (`SUING_REPEAT_COOLDOWN_MS`), so "truce ends, take 3 tiles, get paid" can't repeat every 12h. Any losing AI's own proactive truce offer carries the payment it would make (suing or not); on accept it stands while the AI still counts as losing and can afford it, even if the fast-fading pace has lowered its price since (`ai-offer-recheck.ts`). The trade screen's Suggest deal button fills in the best terms the AI would accept (`suggestedDeal` in `client-diplomacy-trade-view.ts`; for resource trades `suggestedTradeDeal` in `client-diplomacy-trade-balance.ts` tops up the goods already offered, then coin, and asks the AI for any surplus). Opening a good on the trade screen balances the deal in one press: a good on the player's side gets exactly the amount the AI needs to accept (capped at what the player holds), and a good on the AI's side, when the deal already overpays it, gets as much as the surplus pays for (`balancingAmount`, same shared pricing the gateway decides with). The trade screen's Gift option gives an AI coin for nothing back, even during a truce (`DIPLOMACY_GIFT`, `diplomacy-gift-message.ts`): paid through the same settlement as deal terms and remembered as goodwill. Gifts and deal payments form one opinion modifier, 6 points per hour of the AI's own income, so a point costs 10 minutes of it and never less than 2 coin, capped at +25 and fading with a 24h half-life (`diplomacy-gift.ts` in shared, so the screen's "+N opinion" preview matches the gateway). The screen blocks gifts that would add nothing or go past the cap, and Suggest deal fills in the least coin (with a little margin for fading) that makes the AI Pleased. The gift preview only says a gift is enough for an alliance when the AI has no other alliance refusal (bloc size, a recent fight, a broken truce); otherwise it shows that refusal. Only AIs nobody is playing take gifts, one settling at a time per player. A free AI truce offer stands until expiry or combat between the two empires withdraws it; accepting it after login does not recalculate its price or require a fresh AI snapshot. Paid counter-offers and alliance offers are re-weighed when accepted and withdrawn if stale (`ai-offer-recheck.ts`); a settlement the simulation applies after the gateway gave up is refunded (`diplomacy-settlement.ts`). Alliances cost 24h of income at Pleased and nothing at Friendly. Below Pleased, an AI that payments could still lift to Pleased (the +25 goodwill cap allows it) asks tribute instead of refusing: the coin that makes it Pleased (shared `giftToReach`; accepted deal payments count as goodwill) plus the 24h Pleased price, so Suggest deal fills it in and the screen shows the split (`allianceTributeNote`); an AI too far below Pleased for that refuses. AIs propose alliances themselves only when already Pleased (`proactive-ai-diplomacy.ts`). Alliances also need no fighting for a day, no broken truce, and, when the merged bloc would hold 40%+ of the world's LAND tiles as Diplomatic Dominance counts them (controlled tiles, Chancery V doubled; `ALLIANCE_LEAD_REQUIRED_LAND_SHARE`), the AI must lead it by 10%+ in tiles; smaller blocs it joins as a junior partner (Diplomatic Dominance goes to the bloc's largest member; the shares come from the season summary's `landTileCount` and `dominanceTilesByPlayerId`, and without them the AI always demands to lead; sizes from the simulation's cached season leaderboard, since live AI snapshots carry no world status; fetched at most every 30s with a 2s cap, `season-leaderboard-cache.ts`). Size-independent refusals (war, attitude) are reported first. The AI never pays for one (`ai-diplomacy-pricing.ts`, `ai-alliance-bloc.ts`). Every 5 minutes each AI whose bloc holds 45%+ of the world's land, counted the same way (`ALLIANCE_LEAVE_LAND_SHARE`, above the join line so it doesn't flip-flop) gives 24h notice to leave any ally through whom its bloc contains an empire at least as large as itself (`ai-alliance-review.ts`, sizes from the simulation's cached season leaderboard; an AI missing from it neither joins nor leaves).
- **AI temperament**: each `ai-<n>` empire has a fixed diplomatic personality by id (Measured, Merchant, Warlike, Peaceable, Vengeful; `ai-diplomacy-temperament.ts`), shown on the trade screen. It scales base truce price, the opinion and reparation cost of taken tiles, the goodwill a coin buys, starting attitude and peace payments, and Warlike AIs do not offer peace merely for being outmatched. Diplomacy only: it does not change the simulation's military planner.
- **AI stance**: seeded AIs count free + mustered manpower and current FOOD-slot deficits when deciding whether they need peace. Border adjacency wraps across the world seam. Front posture modulates military aggression independently of gateway negotiation.

- **Proactive AI negotiation**: one AI every 15 seconds may propose a 12h truce when exhausted/outmatched or a shared-threat alliance, subject to the same memory and bloc-leadership rules as the trade screen. Exhausted means free + mustered manpower at or below 20% of cap, and it only counts toward peace with a given rival when the AI's next manpower refill isn't within 1h and enough to lift it back over 20%, the rival isn't spent too (its free + mustered manpower is above the AI's and above 20% of its own cap), and the AI isn't boxed in by that rival (has neutral land inside its own reach or barbarian ground next to its territory). Otherwise the AI judges the rival on strength as usual (`apps/realtime-gateway/src/ai-diplomacy-stance/ai-war-exhaustion.ts`). A starving AI still seeks peace. Existing social state owns requests, cooldowns, acceptance and simulation synchronization. Human-controlled seeded identities are excluded.
- **AI FOOD purchases**: on the same 15-second tick, an AI with no spare FOOD slot and no resource offer pending asks another House with spare FOOD supply for 1-3 FOOD slots as an independent resource trade. It pays with a surplus resource slot worth at least as much per day, or a day's value in coin, asks other AIs first and human neighbours after, waits 6h before asking the same House again, and ends each FOOD purchase it made once the day it paid for has passed (supply agreements otherwise never expire), asking again if still short (`apps/realtime-gateway/src/proactive-ai-resource-trade/`). An AI that attacks the House supplying its FOOD loses that supply like any attacker; the attack planner does not weigh this, and the AI asks another House on a later tick.
- **AI surplus sales**: an AI with no FOOD to buy offers a neighbouring player who is out of FOOD, Titanium, Crystal or Umbrite slots (at or below zero headroom) up to 3 of its spare slots, keeping 2 spare for itself (`AI_SALE_RESERVE_SLOTS`). The price is a day's value in coin, or another of the player's spare upkeep slots of equal value when they are short of coin. The offer lapses after an hour; it is not repeated to the same player for 6h, never made to a player it fought within that time, and the AI ends each sale once the paid-for day has passed (`ai-surplus-sale.ts`).

## 9. Seasons and world events

- **Seasons**: time-bounded game instances with `startAt`, `endAt`, `worldSeed`, `techTreeConfigId`, `status`. Single shared season across all players. `packages/shared/src/types.ts:423-430`.
- **Barbarian spawning**: continuous; see §2.
- **Season capacity and the Sector waitlist**: players call a season a **Sector Contest** (player-facing text only, unnumbered so it never clashes with "Sector 003" galaxy numbering; code identifiers, protocol messages, storage and these docs keep "season"). A new spawn is refused with `SEASON_FULL` once `SIMULATION_MAX_SEASON_PLAYERS` humans (default 50; AI empires and returning players with territory are not counted) have joined; guests have a smaller allowance and are sent to sign in. Before that, a signed-in player joining a running Sector Contest sees the **Sector board** instead of a plain join prompt: the current Sector with its human Houses out of the cap (open, or "looks full" with joining still allowed, since a refused join is what opens the waitlist) and the next Sector with its waitlist count, from the public `GET /sector/board` (`apps/realtime-gateway/src/http-routes/register-sector-board-route.ts`, client `client-sector-board/`). The House count is an estimate from the leaderboard minus AI (`ai-<n>`) and barbarians; the cap also counts landless or eliminated humans still on record, so the board reads low (more so late in a contest), never high. Data for another season is ignored, and a failed load retries after 30s. Guests who press Play now skip the board and join directly. The full screen offers the next-Sector waitlist: `GET/POST/DELETE /sector/waitlist` on the gateway, signed-in non-guest accounts only, keyed by the full season's `seasonId` so a rollover starts an empty list and old lists remain as a record of demand (`apps/realtime-gateway/src/http-routes/register-sector-waitlist-routes.ts`, `sector-waitlist-store/`; client `client-sector-waitlist/`). Waitlist rows are personal data: `scripts/account-deletion/erase-account-data.mjs` erases them.
- **Starting the next Sector Contest**: the simulation holds one world, so there is no vote and no clock. Once a season ends (its winner is crowned) the ended world stays loaded for Look Around, and the season-end screen offers **Join the next Sector Contest (n/10)**: `START_NEW_SEASON` without `force` commits the account, stored in the waitlist table under its own key (`<endedSeasonId>:next-contest`), so only presses after the crown count: rows from the full-Sector waitlist never start a world on their own. Guests can't commit and are offered the save-your-empire panel instead. When `NEXT_CONTEST_MIN_COMMITTED` (10) accounts have committed, or an admin long-presses the button (`force: true`, admin only), the gateway rolls the simulation over, re-reads the commitments (Houses can commit while the world is built) and calls JoinSeason for every committed account, online or not, before announcing the new season, so they land together and arrivals from the announcement can't take their slots; a guest online at the start is sent in by the client's guest auto-join. The gateway also checks once at boot and every 15s, so commitments made before a restart still start it. The admin HTTP start route does not join committed accounts. Live count: `SEASON_START_VOTE_UPDATE` broadcast and the INIT fields `seasonStartVoteCount`/`seasonStartVoted`/`seasonStartVoteThreshold` (names kept from the old start vote). Someone who never joined the ended world sees a short result card (winner and top 5) instead of the full ledger. `apps/realtime-gateway/src/next-contest/next-contest.ts`, `packages/client/src/client-next-contest/`
- **Season ratings (ELO)**: at rollover every player on the final leaderboard is rated from their final rank as a pairwise round-robin (beat everyone below, lost to everyone above, drew on shared rank). Start 1200, K=32, averaged over the N−1 opponents so one season moves a rating by at most 32 whatever the field size. Stored on `season_participation` (`rating_before`/`rating_after`); pre-existing seasons are backfilled oldest-first at boot. Shown on the player profile via `/hq/career/by-player/:playerId`. `apps/simulation/src/season-elo/`
- **Client visibility**: season status, victory pressure, leader hold-time, and barbarian positions are streamed live to clients. No hidden standings.

## 11. Strategic snapshot (the meta layer that already exists)

`apps/simulation/src/ai/automation-strategic-snapshot.ts` builds an `AutomationStrategicSnapshot` per planner tick. This is the existing "meta" layer; any new strategic AI work should consume or extend it rather than reinventing it.

- **Victory path selection** (`apps/simulation/src/ai/victory-path-scoring.ts`): scores all 5 paths every tick. The worker planner receives each AI's real 0..1 season-victory progress (`ai-victory-standings.ts`, from `computeSeasonVictory`) and ranks paths by it on a 0..1000 scale. The AI stays on a path while it is a contender (≥72% progress); otherwise it switches when another path is >28 points (2.8%) ahead, or >56 while it is a soft contender (≥58%). The old situational heuristic adds a small rank bonus (≤20, below the repivot margin) that only breaks near-ties, and a contender leaves its path when another path’s condition is already met. Non-leader Economic Hegemony progress is income ÷ leader income ÷ 1.33 so it is on the same scale as the leader’s. Without standings (cold start, non-competitive players, direct planner) it falls back to self-relative heuristics that overstate progress.
- **Stop the leader** (`apps/simulation/src/ai/ai-victory-focus.ts`): every AI reads the same victory objectives, so they all agree on who is about to win. An empire (human or AI) holding a victory condition gets full focus; one at 70%+ progress on a path gets partial focus (up to 60%). AIs score that empire's border tiles higher as attack and muster targets, and get a boost to ATTACK and MUSTER when their chosen target belongs to it. Against an empire holding a condition, AIs also attack on fronts they would otherwise only contain. They still need the manpower, never attack allies or truce partners, and the leader never targets itself.
- **Strategic focus mode**: one of `BALANCED`, `ECONOMIC_RECOVERY`, `ISLAND_FOOTPRINT`, `MILITARY_PRESSURE`, `BORDER_CONTAINMENT`. Controls goal priorities and which actions are filtered out. `:439-460`
- **Front posture**: one of `BREAK`, `CONTAIN`, `TRUCE`. Modulates frontier aggression. `:368-381`
- **ATTACK ⇆ SETTLE gate** (`attackReady`): true only if `canAttack` (coin + manpower) AND `manpowerSufficient` (threat-scaled) AND (`pressureThreatensCore` OR (not `needsFood` AND not `needsEconomy`) OR `pressureAttackScore ≥ 180`). This is the gate the AI tunnel-vision memory refers to. `:13-23, 430-433`, `apps/simulation/src/ai/automation-command-planner.ts:294-297`

## 12. Tile mutation chokepoints

For event-driven indexes (chunk aggregates, focus invalidation, etc.), these are the points where state mutates:

- **Single tile-state chokepoint**: `SimulationRuntime.replaceTileState()` (`apps/simulation/src/runtime/runtime.ts:1539`). ~25 call sites across the runtime funnel through this method. Every authoritative change to ownership, ownershipState, structure, fort, observatory, and siegeBattery goes through here.
- **Validation gate** for player-initiated mutations: `validateFrontierCommand()` (`packages/game-domain/src/index.ts:180-257`).
- **Existing event hooks at the chokepoint**: updates player ownership summaries (`runtime.ts`). No general "tile state mutated" event yet — adding one at this point would catch every relevant change in a single emit.
- **Worker tile caches** (`system-job-worker.ts`, `ai-planner-worker.ts`, `ai-command-producer-worker.ts`, `system-command-producer-worker.ts`) maintain their own replicas of tile state. These are downstream of canonical mutations and should not be hooked for aggregation; subscribe to the runtime emit instead.
- **Barbarian state changes** (walk/multiply) are tile-state mutations + a side-channel `barbarianTileProgress` Map in `SimulationRuntime`. The tile-state part is covered by the chokepoint; progress is internal to the runtime.

## 13. Performance constraints

- The simulation runs in a single Node.js event loop. **Synchronous CPU work on the planner main loop blocks user-facing actions** (auth, build commands, etc.). The "AUTH→INIT exceeded threshold" Slack alert (#211) exists because this has happened in prod.
- The AI planner is the dominant CPU consumer. As of 2026-05-14, single-player planner stalls of 30–45s have been observed in prod, traced to `analyzeOwnedFrontierTargetsFromLookup` (`apps/simulation/src/ai/frontier-command-planner.ts:248-312`) running a `O(owned_tiles × candidates_per_origin)` enumeration with no cap. With ~1000 owned tiles, ~15–20 candidates per origin, and a broad-pass fallback that can double the loop, worst case is millions of tile-map lookups per planner tick.
- **AGENTS.md** "AI CPU Guardrails" forbids calling heavy concrete selectors (`bestAiSettlementTile`, etc.) from snapshot or planning-static cache builders. Honor that — it exists for a reason.

## 14. Things that look like signals but aren't

- **`actionKey` in `ai budget breach` logs** is the action the planner *selected*, not where time was spent. Time is spent in candidate enumeration before action selection. Don't infer "this action is slow"; infer "the planner ran the full enumeration this turn."
- **"Functional AI is the new variable"**: AI has been alive since #177; it is not a recently-introduced unknown. Dead-AI behavior was the pre-#177 split-process deployment.
- **"Domination income"**: see §6 — there's no such income mechanic.

## Season records

- **Longest Road** records the owning empire, length in tiles, and the names and coordinates of both endpoint towns/cities. Only settled land belonging to the same empire connects the road; the endpoints must be towns of at least Town tier. The final-map road network supplies the record when the season ends.
- **Most Deadly Tile** records the tile coordinates, total combat manpower spent attacking it, and the empire with the largest cumulative loss there, including that empire's loss count. Repeated attacks accumulate across the season and persist across restarts. This counts actual combat manpower losses, rather than expansion costs or a fictional defender casualty estimate.
- The crowned records are carried through login and season archives. Legacy records can gain road details from the frozen final map when the road length still matches the captured record. Retained combat logs can recover older player attribution when their losses cover the entire saved tile total and that total has complete season-history provenance. Older casualty totals without complete player attribution are labelled unavailable and offer a bug-report button; recent losses alone never establish a season-wide player record.
- Per-player casualty attribution is bounded to 100,000 tile/player entries per runtime and 256 players per tile, with entry-count and cap-hit gauges logged on the existing persistence cadence. It is saved with the existing top 200 deadliest tiles outside the world snapshot. The existing top-200 restart tradeoff remains: lower-ranked tiles resume from zero after a restart. Truncated saves retain provenance: a tile omitted at restart cannot claim a complete player-loss record if it later becomes the deadliest tile, including across further restarts. If attribution reaches a cap, aggregate totals continue, and incomplete tiles do not claim a player record.
